The principle

The owner travels through bad-internet areas and the phone app at /m is his most-used surface on the road. Before this mission a bad connection cleared things out — the session list vanished, voice disappeared. The rule applied everywhere now: never clear good data just because the connection is bad. Every surface keeps its last-known content, and one small banner at the top names the bad connection so he knows it is the network, not the fleet.

Phase 1 — the connection-health signal and the one banner MERGED & LIVE

PR #1374 · commit 70c5b2f8

A shared client-core health store (connection/health.ts) that every Gateway contact reports into, fed at ONE transport choke point — a gatewayFetch wrapper in api/client.ts that all reads and writes route through — reusing the existing issue #1028 unreachable taxonomy. A useConnectionHealth() hook drives one global banner mounted once in GatedLayout: hidden while good; when bad, a slim amber strip pins to the top of every screen and says how stale things are (“updated 40s ago”). The roster’s own offline strip was retired so the condition has exactly one voice.

Live proof: airplane mode on the roster, chat, voice, and terminal — content stays on all four, one banner names the bad connection, and it clears within a poll tick of the network returning.
[ owner phone screenshot: banner over the roster in airplane mode ]

Phase 2 — keep-and-mark unreachable machines on the roster MERGED & LIVE

PR #1380 · commit 77c199d1

The roster moved to the /sessions envelope (per-Director reachability) and a new pure merge (fleet/rosterRetention.ts) keeps a last-known-sessions cache per owning Director. Sessions of a Wobbly/Offline machine STAY on the roster — grayed, dashed, with a plain note (“Unreachable — MACHINE — last seen 2m ago”) — retained even after the Gateway’s grace window drops them. A session is removed ONLY when its owning Director answers Online without it. No shrink heuristics.

Live proof: stop a second machine’s Director — its sessions stay, grayed and named unreachable; kill a session on a reachable Director — it leaves promptly; restart the stopped Director — its cards return to live in place.
[ owner phone screenshot: a grayed unreachable machine’s sessions kept on the roster ]

Phase 3 — terminal reconnect that never blanks MERGED & LIVE

PR #1382 · commit 2ea19f11

The live terminal mirror used to wipe the screen on every failed reconnect attempt (term.reset() ran at the top of connect(), which the ~1200ms retry loop calls repeatedly). The reset moved into the socket’s onopen, right before the byte-0 history replay — so a failed attempt keeps the last-known screen and only a real replay clears it. The socket also feeds the global banner (a dead stream shows it even when polls succeed), and a “Reconnecting to the live terminal…” note floats over the last-known screen. Chat keeps its bubbles and a failing history poll now surfaces through the banner.

Live proof: airplane mode mid-stream — the terminal keeps its content (never blank), the banner and reconnecting note show; on reconnect it replays and is live again with no user action; chat keeps its bubbles throughout.
[ owner phone screenshot: terminal keeping its content with the reconnecting note ]

Phase 4 — hardening (poll timeout, banner debounce) MERGED & LIVE

PR #1384 · commit 517ed198

The repeating poll reads (roster, chat, voice) pass a 10s timeout through gatewayFetch, so a request that hangs on a half-open mobile connection is aborted and reported unreachable instead of sitting in flight forever. The banner is debounced (2.5s) so a one-packet blip — a single failed poll, or the terminal stream’s reconnect blipping while polls still succeed — never flashes it; recovery is instant. Voice playback and dictation review were exercised, not rebuilt: voice plays only a fully-buffered local clip and dictation is the durable store-and-forward path, both already drop-safe.

Live proof: a real drive or cottage visit — roster, chat, terminal, and voice all stay populated and honest, with the one banner the only sign of trouble, and it does not twitch on a passing blip.
