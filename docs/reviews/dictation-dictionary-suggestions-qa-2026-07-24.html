Dictation dictionary suggestions

The Gateway keeps each tenant's dictation transcripts, mines the words the speech model keeps getting wrong that are not yet in the dictionary, and offers them as one-press dictionary additions - on the Dictionary page and over the API. This report is the evidence that the feature is built and works. It is written to be read before a pull request is opened: nothing here has been merged or deployed.

Verdict: Built and proven for the whole of GitHub issue #2075. 34 new automated tests pass; both database migrations generate and their model snapshots are in sync; the Cockpit builds; the real page renders correctly against live-shaped data (screenshots below). Two pieces of the broader mockup - the Settings privacy screen and the daily-email block - are deliberately out of scope here (they belong to issue #2074); see Scope.

What was built

The storage foundation (per-tenant dictation_transcripts table) shipped separately in PR #2093. This mission built everything that reads it and turns it into suggestions:

Server (mining, storage, API)

  • The miner (MistranscriptionMiner, in CcDirector.Core) - a pure, static, language-agnostic function that clusters phonetically-near spellings across a tenant's raw transcripts (single-linkage over the existing Jaro metric), takes the most-frequent spelling of a cluster as the canonical term and the rest as its wrong spellings, applies strict evidence thresholds, and excludes anything already in the glossary or dismissed. Same design as the shipped FuzzyDictionaryMatcher: no I/O, no model, fully unit-testable.
  • The dismissal store - a per-tenant dictation_suggestion_dismissals table (SQLite + Postgres migrations) holding each dismissed term with a snapshot of its evidence, so the "Dismissed terms" screen can still explain a term after its transcripts age out.
  • The suggestion service - wires the three tenant-scoped inputs (transcripts, glossary, dismissals) into the miner, caches the result per tenant on a 2-minute TTL, and invalidates on any change.
  • The API - six routes under /ingest/dictionary, sharing the exact tenant-resolution idiom of the existing glossary routes (403 when no tenant resolves, never the Local partition on hosted): read suggestions, read the count, apply, dismiss, list dismissed, restore.

Cockpit (the page)

  • A suggestions panel above Vocabulary with pre-ticked checkboxes, per-row evidence and counts, per-row Dismiss, and one green "Add N selected" button.
  • The quiet zero state when nothing is pending, and a collapsible "Dismissed terms" section with Restore.
  • A red count badge on the Dictionary nav item, driven by the Gateway-computed count (the client renders the number, it never re-derives it - rule 7, the client is dumb).

Scope: what is deliberately not here

The approved mockup (docs/reviews/dictation-dictionary-suggestions-mockup-v2-2026-07-24.html) draws five screens and closes with four open decisions. This mission implemented issue #2075 in full. Two things it shows belong to a different issue and were left out on purpose:

  • Deferred Settings privacy screen (mockup screen 4) - the retention toggle, the off-means-delete opt-out, and the "delete all transcripts now" control. The 30-day / 10,000-row retention itself already ships (PR #2093); the settings surface for it is separate work.
  • Deferred Daily-email block (mockup screen 5) - the mockup itself attributes this to issue #2074, a separate issue with its own email cadence decision.

Both are honest follow-ups, not gaps in #2075's acceptance. Flagging them here so the merge decision is made with the boundary visible.

Decisions taken on the four open questions

  • Evidence threshold: started strict, as recommended - a term must be heard wrong at least 3 times and at least 25% of the times it was said. Both are tunable constants.
  • Does Add write corrections too? Yes - one press writes the term into Vocabulary and its observed wrong spellings into Common mistranscriptions (proven by the HTTP test).
  • Default-on storage: unchanged from PR #2093 (stored by default, 30-day window). The opt-out control is the deferred Settings screen.
  • Email cadence: deferred with the email block (issue #2074).

Known limitations (by design)

  • Single-word terms only. The miner suggests one-word terms - "mindzie", "ConPty", "Frederiksen" (three of the four real examples). A two-word term ("Center Consulting") is a documented follow-up: gluing a term to a neighbouring word needs a way to reject function-word neighbours, and doing that without a per-language stop list is real work a noisy first cut would get wrong. The miner stays conservative rather than emitting spurious multi-word rows.
  • Canonical = most-frequent spelling. The canonical term is the plurality spelling in a cluster (the form the model gets right when the audio is clear). If wrong spellings ever out-number the correct one as a single form, the canonical could be off - the customer simply unticks or dismisses that row. This matches the real data, where the correct spelling is the most frequent.
