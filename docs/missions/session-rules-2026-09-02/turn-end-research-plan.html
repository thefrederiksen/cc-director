The turn end: what happens when a session goes quiet

Research plan, 4 September 2026. Written after the owner asked to stop patching the rules engine and look at the whole moment as one thing. Every number here was measured today from the running hosted Gateway, not estimated.

What happens first

Build the logging harness. Record every real turn end on the fleet - the screen, what each judgement would have decided, and what should have happened - and tune against that instead of against argument.

Everything else in this plan is a question that harness answers. We currently reason from 32 screens captured once in June. The fleet produces about 400 turn ends a day, and none of them are being kept.

Cost is settled and is no longer an argument. The smart model on every single turn end costs us about $12 a month at today's rate, $19 at list, for the heaviest account on the fleet. It is an included service, so we absorb it - and it becomes part of Pro.

What the owner has settled

  • Cost is not the blocker. ~$12 to $19 a month per heavy account, absorbed by us.
  • This is a Pro feature.
  • The rules engine goes keyword-first. If a rule watches for something searchable, search for it; wake a model only when the words are actually on the screen.
  • "Does this session need me" cannot be keyword-gated, and we pay for it anyway. There is no word to search for - a session quietly waiting on you looks exactly like one that finished. This is the judgement that takes load off the owner's head, and it is worth the money.
  • Retry cadence and give-up: try every 15 minutes, give up after 6 hours, expressed as a duration rather than a count (ruling G1). The shipped supervisor independently arrived at the same 15 minutes.

The five research areas

A. The logging harness Do this first

What actually happens at the end of a real turn, at a scale where the answer means something?

Record every turn end on the fleet: the screen at that moment, which judgements ran, what each decided, how long it took, what it cost - and, crucially, what should have happened, so the record can be replayed as a test set that grows by itself.

This replaces a 32-case corpus captured once with roughly 400 real cases a day. Every other question below is answered by replaying it. Retention is already ruled: short, and scoped to the account, the same as turns.

B. When is a turn actually over, and when does the session wake?

Ten seconds of quiet works well. So why can a snooze never outlast half an hour?

Some agents tap a byte into the terminal every thirty minutes. We read that as activity and wake out of a snooze for nothing. That is agent-specific knowledge, and it belongs in the driver that wraps each agent rather than in generic code.

The boundary to hold: the driver should produce better FACTS - this agent is genuinely idle, that byte was a heartbeat and not work. The Gateway keeps doing the RULING. An edge that starts deciding things renders what is plausible rather than what is true.

C. The cheap gate

How much can be decided for nothing, before any model is involved?

The supervisor already does this and the numbers are stark: 10 model calls in two days against about 1,200 turn ends, because a word table decides almost everything for free. Its table is not naive - it looks only at the last few lines of real content, and it requires an error marker beside ambiguous words, so a session merely discussing a log is never typed into.

Those are exactly the confusions that beat our model. Run that classifier against the 32-case corpus and find out how much of our problem it already solves. That is a harness run, and the harness exists.

D. One call or several, and how small can the question be?

Can the judgements share a prompt, and must we really send forty lines of screen?

We currently send the last 40 real lines - a measured median of 3,940 characters, about 1,100 tokens. The owner's question is whether a much smaller slice would do.

The caution, learned this week: a question that asks two things returns one answer and you cannot tell which it answered. That fault is already in the round A design and it is why n11 and n18, the same shape of screen, got different verdicts. So if judgements share a call, they must return separately named, separately auditable answers - never one blurred verdict.

And they gate differently. The rules question can be word-gated; the needs-me question cannot. Merging them drags the cheap one up to the expensive one's frequency.

E. What the judgement should be, now that the supervisor exists

If a working feature already recovers stuck sessions, what are rules FOR?

The supervisor recovers from faults the product knows about - its signatures are ours, its text is always continue, and an account cannot change any of it. Rules let an account say in its own words what to watch for and what to type.

They share the whole spine: the turn-end trigger, the screen read, the fault gate, the wait, the re-read before typing, the menu refusal, the ceiling, the recorded log. Rules rebuilt a thinner version of each. The question is whether rules should become the account-authored layer on top of the supervisor rather than a parallel machine beside it.

One thing still to confirm

The owner has said "transcription" several times. Transcription is a voice turned into text - a different provider, audio in, text out - and it cannot share a prompt with a judgement about a screen. The turn summary, the short description of what a session just did, absolutely can, because it reads the same screen. This plan assumes the summary was meant.
