How a rule gets made, how a rule runs, and why they time out

Measured on 3 September 2026 against the live hosted model, using the product's own code. Everything on this page is from a run, not an estimate.

The headline, and it is worse than I said earlier. The timeouts are not caused by the size of the prompt going in - I claimed that without measuring it, and it is wrong. They are caused by how much the model has to WRITE back. And it is not only the authoring call: the call a live rule makes every time it fires has the same problem. On the current model, roughly one in three of those runs out of time and the rule does nothing.

3. Why they time out

Two candidate causes were being confused: how much the model has to READ, and how much it has to WRITE. Holding one fixed and varying the other separates them. Three runs each, same endpoint, same model, same code path as production.

Is it the size of the question?

It is not the prompt size. A question as long as ours, answered in one word, comes back in four seconds. A one-line question that asks for 400 words back never finishes inside the limit. The model is slow at writing, and both of our calls ask it to write five to eight hundred characters of JSON.

The answers were the same size in every row - about 500 to 760 characters. The thinking model writes them at roughly 15 to 20 characters a second, which puts a 600-character answer right on top of a 60-second deadline. The fast model does the identical work in 8 to 14 seconds, every time.

Where the 60 seconds came from. It is HostedInferenceBrain's deadline, written for the voice path, where a real call takes ten to twenty seconds. It was never calibrated for a call that has to write a paragraph of JSON. The client also asks for the whole answer at once (stream = false), so every second of writing counts against it.

4. So what do we do

You were right that this should not be committed as it stands. Not because the authoring is broken - it works - but because the measurement above says a live rule fails about one time in three, and that is the feature, not a rough edge on it.

There are three ways out, and the third is the one I would not take:

  • Move both calls to the fast model. Measured: 8 to 14 seconds, no failures. It is the obvious fix for MAKING a rule and I would do it today. For RUNNING a rule it is a real product decision and yours, not mine: the thinking model was chosen deliberately, because judging whether an instruction reaches a screen is the thing that stops a rule acting on a screen that merely mentions the words. That judgement is what made the negative control work. Trading it for speed needs the negative control re-run on the fast model before anyone believes it still holds.
  • Raise the deadline for these two calls. Honest and small, and it does not touch which model judges a screen. It makes a slow case slower to sit through rather than failing, which is the right trade for a rule running at 3am and the wrong one for a person waiting at a keyboard.
  • Ask the model to write less. Cheapest to do and the one I would refuse: most of what it writes back is the reason and the read-back, and those ARE the product. Cutting them to fit a deadline would buy speed by deleting the record.

What I would do, if you want a recommendation. Fast model for MAKING a rule, now - it is a person waiting at a keyboard and there is no judgement at stake. Raise the deadline for RUNNING a rule rather than changing its model, and put the fast model for that path behind a measured re-run of the negative control. That way nothing about how a rule decides changes without evidence.

What this page does not tell you

  • Three runs per row, on one machine, on one afternoon. It is enough to separate reading from writing and to rank the two models; it is not a latency budget.
  • The fast model was measured for SPEED only. Nothing here says its answers are as good - the negative control (a rule declining a screen that carries its own trigger words) has not been re-run on it.
  • Why the thinking model is this slow is not established here. That is on the other side of our own hosted endpoint and has not been looked at.
