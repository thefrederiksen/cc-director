Give your agents a browser of their own

A browser here is a real, signed-in browser your agents can drive — click things, read pages, fill forms — through Browser Harness. It is not a scraper and not a headless shell. It is Chrome or Edge, running on this machine, that an agent controls like a person would.

It is not your everyday browser, and that is deliberate. Chrome and Edge now refuse to be remote-controlled on your main profile, and they encrypt its saved logins so they cannot be copied out. So DevThrottle creates a separate, dedicated profile instead — which is also the arrangement you want: your agent gets exactly the accounts you give it, and nothing else.

1

You create it

DevThrottle makes a fresh, dedicated browser profile in its own folder. Your everyday Chrome and Edge are never touched.

2

You sign it in, once

It opens and you log in by hand — DevThrottle never types your credentials. The login then lasts for months.

3

Any agent attaches

From then on any agent on this machine drives it with one command. No sign-in, no re-auth, no captcha loop.

Make one for each role you want an agent to act as

The useful way to think about it is to mirror the separation you already keep in your own head — work, personal, a particular client. Name each one after the identity it holds, and you always know what an agent can reach when you point it at that browser.

It starts completely empty. A new browser here is signed in to nothing at all — it does not inherit your existing tabs, bookmarks or logins. You choose what goes in it: sign in to a Google or Microsoft account to bring a whole profile across, or just sign in to the individual websites you want the agent to reach. Whatever you sign in is exactly what your agents can drive — nothing else.

Takes about a minute, most of it you signing in.
