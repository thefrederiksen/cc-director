  First, a correction to how I put this earlier. I said we were losing money on it. Per token that's true, but I checked the actual traffic and the model as a whole is not losing money, so don't decide this as if moneyispouringout.     

  What's actually true: the output row of its price card is under cost. Output costs us $0.55 per million tokens and we charge $0.20 — about
  a third of cost. But the way the model actually gets used is very input-heavy, and input is priced correctly at 2x. Over the last ninety  
  days it took 2.9 million input tokens against 415 thousand output tokens, charged $0.99, and truly cost us $0.49. That is exactly the 2x  
  we aim for. The broken row is being hidden by the shape of the usage.

  So the exposure is not today's bill, it's the shape of a future one: anyone who used it for long generated output — writing documents,    
  generating code at length — would flip it into a loss, and nothing would flag that.

  The fact that makes this easy: you are the only account that has ever called it. 1,542 calls in ninety days, all from
  soren@centerconsulting.com. Nobody else has touched it. So "prices only move down after publication" has no injured party here — there is 
  no customer who would see a price rise.

  My recommendation is to drop it from the catalog, for a reason beyond the price. It failed the benchmark this morning badly: 200 seconds  
  and more on tasks GLM-5.2 finished in ten to twenty, it timed out entirely on the rename task, and it is the one model we serve that gets 
  no prompt caching at all, so every turn of an agent loop re-bills the whole conversation at full rate. It is a model we would never       
  recommend to anyone. Keeping a model on the card that we would steer people away from, and whose price card is broken, costs more in      
  explaining than it earns.

  The alternatives are fair though: raise the output price to $1.10, which is honest and costs nobody anything since only you use it. Or    
  leave it exactly as it is and accept a bounded, currently-theoretical exposure.
